What we actually do

Books kept per cabin

Gross bookings, platform fees, cleaning, management, supplies, repairs and debt service tracked against the specific cabin that produced them. A full calendar and a profitable cabin are not the same thing, and only one of them shows up here.

Payouts untangled

Based in East Tennessee

We live where your cabin is.

Sevierville, Gatlinburg and Pigeon Forge sit in our own service area, and the local filings are their own small trap. Sevier County charges a 3% lodging tax on overnight rentals outside the city limits, due monthly by the 20th, and it applies to your cleaning and pet fees, not just the nightly rate. Inside Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge, Sevierville or Pittman Center, the city takes its own instead.

It is just quietly missed, usually by an owner who lives somewhere else and assumed the platform handled everything.

Answers before you ask.

Is my cabin income Schedule E or Schedule C?

Usually Schedule E. It moves to Schedule C, and picks up self-employment tax, when you provide substantial services of the kind a hotel provides: housekeeping during the stay, meals, a concierge. Standard cleaning between guests is not substantial services. The distinction is worth real money, because Schedule C income carries roughly 15.3% self-employment tax that Schedule E income does not.

What is the 7-day rule everyone talks about?

Under the passive activity regulations, an activity is not treated as a rental activity when the average period of customer use is seven days or less. Most Smokies cabins clear that easily on weekend and long-weekend stays. On its own it changes nothing. It only matters alongside the next answer.

Can my cabin losses offset my W-2 income?

They can, if the average stay is seven days or less and you materially participate in the activity. Meet both and the losses are non-passive, which is what lets them offset wages or other income. You do not need real estate professional status for this; that is a separate rule people routinely confuse it with. What you do need is contemporaneous records of your hours. This is fact-specific and it is worth deciding before the year is over, not after.

Schedule E versus C, passive versus non-passive: are those the same question?

No, and conflating them is the most common mistake we see. Schedule E or C decides whether you owe self-employment tax. Passive or non-passive decides whether a loss can offset your other income. A cabin can sit on Schedule E and still produce non-passive losses. Both questions have to be answered, separately.

Do I still owe Sevier County lodging tax if Airbnb collects it?

If the platform is remitting it, you are not filing that return again. You are still expected to open a lodging account with the County Trustee, and to hold a business license and a state sales tax registration. Inside the city limits of Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge, Sevierville or Pittman Center the city administers its own tax instead of the county.

Do you do cost segregation studies?

No. That is specialist engineering work and we do not pretend otherwise. For a cabin that has just gone into service it is often worth doing, and it pairs with the rule above. We keep the books, file the returns, and work alongside whoever performs the study.

Do you track each cabin separately?

Yes. Every cabin gets its own tracking in QuickBooks so bookings, platform fees, cleaning and management land against the property that produced them. Reported as one lump, the books tell you the portfolio made money. They do not tell you which cabin is carrying the others.
