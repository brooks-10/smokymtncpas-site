Behind on the Books and Afraid to Look: A Cleanup Story

Smoky Mountain CPAs

A service business owner came to us a few months ago carrying the same feeling a lot of business owners carry: he knew his books were behind, and he was scared of exactly how behind they were.

He’d been keeping up with his bookkeeping software the best he could, but a few things had slipped. A setup call for one of his tracked accounts never got rescheduled, so close to a thousand dollars of small cash transactions sat untouched for months. Personal card charges for the business were uploaded but never sorted. Payroll was posting as one lump number instead of breaking down by employee, so he couldn’t tell what he actually owed anyone. And a couple of months of transactions weren’t in the system at all. He’d been keeping them on a spreadsheet that never quite got finished.

“I felt like I had no idea what was actually true” is the kind of thing we hear a lot. Not because the owner wasn’t trying. Because bookkeeping software will happily show you a number that has nothing to do with what your bank account actually says, and most owners have no easy way to know the difference.

What we found

We started the way we always start: we pulled every real bank statement, month by month, and checked it against the books line by line. Not what the software guessed. Not what a prior bookkeeper typed in. The actual bank statements.

What we found was familiar. Close to a year of months that had never been truly reconciled to the bank. A couple of months of transactions missing entirely, which we rebuilt from scratch off the real statements once we had them in hand. A stack of small check payments with no payee attached (his bank simply doesn’t print who a numbered check went to), so we set them aside honestly rather than guess, and flagged them for him to identify from his own records. And a chunk of what looked like business debt on the balance sheet that, once we traced its full history, turned out to be the owner’s own money moving in and out, not a real loan at all, just mislabeled.

What we did

We rebuilt the missing months transaction by transaction, using the real statements. We reconciled every month, all the way back to when the account opened, to the penny, against the actual bank documents, not an estimate. We walked every line of his balance sheet, checking each account for whether the number on the page actually meant what it claimed to mean, and cleaned up what we found.

What’s different now

His checking account ties to the bank every month, to the penny. The mislabeled debt that was inflating his balance sheet got straightened out. And instead of a once a year scramble to figure out what’s true, he’s on a monthly rhythm now: transactions get coded as they come in, the books close as soon as the statement is available, and he gets a plain language update every month instead of a mystery.
