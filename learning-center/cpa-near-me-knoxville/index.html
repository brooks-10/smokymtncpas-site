How to find a CPA near you in Knoxville

Type "CPA near me" into a search bar and you get a map, a row of ads, and a stack of national directories. None of them tell you which firm is right for you. This is the checklist we would hand a friend in Knoxville or anywhere in East Tennessee: what a CPA actually does, how that differs from a bookkeeper or a tax preparer, what drives the cost, what to ask on the first call, and the warning signs that should send you back to the search results.

The short answer

Start with what you need done, not with who is closest. Books, a tax return, year-round planning and a cleanup of messy books are different jobs, and not every firm does all of them. Then confirm the license, ask who will actually do the work, and get the scope and price in writing before you hand anything over. Being near you matters less than it used to. Knowing Tennessee tax matters more.

CPA, tax preparer, enrolled agent or bookkeeper?

Most people searching for an accountant near them are really looking for one of four people. The titles sound alike. The licenses behind them are not.

Bookkeeper

Records your transactions, reconciles your accounts and keeps the books current. No license is required. A good bookkeeper is worth a lot, but bookkeeping and tax are separate skills.

Tax preparer

Anyone who prepares federal returns for pay needs a Preparer Tax Identification Number (PTIN) from the IRS. That is the only federal requirement. Many work seasonally, and an uncredentialed preparer has very limited rights to speak for you if the IRS has questions.

Enrolled agent (EA)

Licensed by the IRS after passing its exam, and focused on tax. An EA can represent you before the IRS on any tax matter. EAs do not usually keep books or prepare financial statements.

Certified public accountant (CPA)

Licensed by the state after a degree, the CPA exam and supervised experience, with continuing education every year. A CPA can prepare returns, represent you before the IRS, and also handle books, financial statements and planning.

So, does a CPA do taxes? Many do, and some do not. Some CPA firms only do audits or corporate work. Ask before you assume. The useful question is not "which title is best" but "who here will understand my whole picture, books and taxes together."

When you actually need a CPA, and when you do not

An honest answer saves you money. If your return is one W-2, the standard deduction and nothing else, tax software or a free IRS volunteer (VITA) site will probably serve you fine. Paying a CPA for that is optional.

A CPA earns the fee when any of these are true:

  • You own a business, an LLC or an S corporation.
  • You own rental property or a short-term rental cabin.
  • You sold a business, a property or a large investment this year.
  • You are behind on filings, or you got a letter from the IRS or the Tennessee Department of Revenue.
  • Your books are months behind or nobody trusts the numbers.
  • You want someone to plan with during the year, not just file in April.

TurboTax or an accountant? Software does exactly what you tell it. It will not ask whether your single-member LLC owes Tennessee franchise and excise tax, or whether your cabin needs its own sales and occupancy tax filings. A CPA is paid to ask the questions you did not know to ask.

Why local still matters in Tennessee

Tennessee has no state income tax on wages, and the old Hall tax on interest and dividends ended for tax years starting in 2021. That leads a lot of owners, and a lot of out-of-state preparers, to assume there is nothing to file here. For a business, that is wrong.

  • Franchise and excise tax. Most LLCs, corporations and limited partnerships owe it, including many single-member LLCs the IRS ignores for income tax. Excise tax is 6.5% of net earnings, and franchise tax is based on net worth with an annual minimum. A missed return here is one of the most common surprises we see.
  • Business tax. A tax on gross receipts, owed to the state and to most cities and counties. A return is required once your receipts in a jurisdiction reach $100,000.
  • Sales tax. 7% state rate plus a local rate of up to 2.75%. Online sellers also need to know which marketplaces collect and remit for them.
  • Short-term rentals. Cabins in Gatlinburg, Pigeon Forge and Sevierville carry sales tax and local occupancy tax on top of the federal return. We wrote a full guide to short-term rental taxes in Tennessee.

Can you use a remote CPA? Yes. Most of the work runs fine through secure document sharing, and plenty of our own clients never visit the office. What you want is a CPA who knows Tennessee filings and is reachable when you need them, wherever they sit.

What drives the cost

We are not going to put a number here, because any number given before someone looks at your file is a guess. These are the things that move a CPA's fee, so you can compare quotes fairly:

  • How many returns and entities. A Schedule C on your personal return is a different job from an S corporation return plus two personal returns.
  • What is inside them. K-1s, more than one state, rental properties, depreciation, a sale of property.
  • The condition of your books. Clean, reconciled books make a return faster. Messy books mean cleanup first, and that is a separate project.
  • Prior years. Unfiled returns or old notices add work.
  • Once a year or all year. Filing in April costs less than planning with you every quarter, and also does less.
  • Fixed fee or hourly. Ask which, and ask what happens when the job grows.

Many CPAs, us included, offer a free first call. A real quote should come after someone has seen last year's return or your books. Our tax page explains what moves a return quote, and what bookkeeping costs in Knoxville covers the books side.

Questions to ask a CPA before you hire

  1. Are you licensed, and is the firm licensed? Then check it yourself (see below).
  2. Who will actually do my work, and who reviews it? The partner you meet is not always the person preparing the return.
  3. Do you work with businesses like mine? Real estate, ecommerce, contractors and rentals each have their own traps.
  4. What exactly is included in the fee, and what costs extra? Get it in an engagement letter.
  5. Who keeps the books, and are they reconciled before the return is prepared? A return is only as good as the books under it. More on that in does your accountant check your books before taxes.
  6. How do we communicate, and how fast do you reply?
  7. When do you need my documents, and what happens if I am late?
  8. What happens if I get an IRS or Tennessee letter?
  9. Will you help me plan during the year, or only file?
  10. If I leave, how do I get my records? A good firm answers this without flinching. Here is how switching accountants actually works.

Red flags

  • They will not give you a license number, or the license is not active.
  • They promise a bigger refund than anyone else, or base the fee on the size of your refund. The IRS warns against both.
  • They will not sign the return as the paid preparer, or ask you to sign a blank return.
  • They want your refund deposited into their account.
  • There is no written engagement letter, so nobody knows what is included.
  • They quote a complex job without looking at anything.
  • They disappear after April 15 and nobody answers until next February.
  • They have never heard of Tennessee franchise and excise tax.

How to check a CPA's license in Tennessee

CPAs here are licensed by the Tennessee State Board of Accountancy, part of the Department of Commerce and Insurance, which runs a free online license lookup. Search the person's name and confirm the status is active. CPA firms practicing in Tennessee also hold a firm permit with the same board. For a CPA licensed in another state, the national CPAverify database searches most states at once. For an enrolled agent, ask for the enrollment card or number.

Common Questions

Answers before you ask.

What is the difference between a tax preparer and a CPA?

A paid tax preparer only needs a Preparer Tax Identification Number (PTIN) from the IRS. A CPA is licensed by the state after a degree, the CPA exam and supervised experience, completes continuing education every year, and can represent you before the IRS on any matter. A CPA can also keep your books, prepare financial statements and plan with you during the year.

Does a CPA do taxes?

Many CPAs prepare business and personal tax returns, and some do not. Some CPA firms only do audits or corporate accounting. Ask before you assume, and ask whether the same firm keeps your books, because a return is only as good as the books under it.

What is the difference between a CPA and an enrolled agent?

An enrolled agent is licensed by the IRS and focuses on tax. A CPA is licensed by the state and covers tax plus accounting, books and financial statements. Both can represent you before the IRS. If you only need a return, either can work. If you need books and tax handled together, a CPA firm is usually the better fit.

Is it worth paying a CPA instead of using TurboTax?

If your return is one W-2 and the standard deduction, software is usually fine. If you own a business, an LLC, rental property or a short-term rental, sold property, or are behind on filings, a CPA usually pays for the fee by catching what software does not ask about, such as Tennessee franchise and excise tax.

How do I find a good CPA for my small business?

Decide what you need first: books, tax, planning or cleanup. Then confirm the license with the Tennessee State Board of Accountancy, ask who will do the work and who reviews it, ask whether they work with businesses like yours, and get the scope and fee in a written engagement letter.

Do I need a CPA near me, or can I use a remote CPA?

Either works. Most CPA work runs well through secure document sharing. What matters more than distance is that the CPA knows Tennessee filings such as franchise and excise tax, business tax and sales tax, and answers when you need them.

Do CPAs offer a free consultation?

Many do. Smoky Mountain CPAs offers a free 30-minute call with a CPA. A firm quote should come after someone has looked at last year's return or your books, not before.
